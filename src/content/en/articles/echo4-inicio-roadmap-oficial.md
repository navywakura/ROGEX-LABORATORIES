# ECHO-4 begins: a self, another and a shared history

21 September 2026 · RxLabs® · Official start of development

<p class="article-status"><strong>ECHO-4 officially enters development.</strong> WORLD-1 provides an executable foundation. E4-SENSATION-1 is next. We are publishing the expanded roadmap: perception, causal influence, self-representation, continuity and learning with a counterpart.</p>

Imagine two agents with different experiences. One has seen where a resource is; the other can open access to it. Solving the task requires discovering what they can do, what their partner does not know and how to coordinate. Then we exchange their capabilities. Could they notice and reorganise?

That scene describes a question we want to implement and measure. **ECHO-4 will investigate how an agent represents its body, another agent and their shared experiences, and when interaction improves outcomes.**

## Why this research matters

The same signal can mean different things depending on who receives it and what happened before. An action that worked yesterday may fail because the body changed, the environment changed or a partner stopped responding as expected. We want mechanisms that make these differences experimentally accessible.

The project's philosophy proposes that functional identity develops through body, memory and interaction. Participatory sense-making offers a framework for asking how a relationship transforms its participants' activity. We will translate that into specific predictions and comparisons. [De Jaegher and Di Paolo](https://hannedejaegher.net/wp-content/uploads/2009/11/dejaegherdipaolo07participatorysensemaking.pdf).

The anonymous story of John Doe and Jane Roe in our [earlier article](/en/articulos/echo4-ego-funcional) expressed that intuition. Antilife supplied an image of counterparts and boundaries. The programme now specifies which observations, actions and outcomes can support a conclusion.

## The official roadmap

1. **WORLD → SENSATION → BOUNDARY → SELF.** Begin with a virtual body. Investigate what sensory history reveals, what changes an action causes and how to distinguish bodily, external or unknown alterations. Prediction, control and bodily membership receive separate tests.

2. **CONTINUITY → MAINTAIN.** Introduce continuity of memory and time, preservation of capabilities, repair and useful work. Remembering must have measurable consequences for what the agent can do later.

3. **OTHER → INTERACTION → RELATION → ROLES.** Add a counterpart with its own information, useful signals, encounter memory and flexible coordination. Measure when cooperation helps, who benefits and what happens when capabilities change.

4. **INTEGRATE.** Test everything together in new scenarios, removing capabilities to measure their contributions. Physical validation follows. **METAVERSE-1 comes last**, representing and investigating results in 3D.

The [official ECHO-4 documentation](/en/docs/echoai/echo4) details all thirteen stages, their status and acceptance requirements.

## Where we actually start

WORLD-1 already has a body, resources and executable rules. Its report documents 48,006 examined transitions and 93 new and selected regression tests. A sustainable trajectory exists, but the initial agent dies from heat after 37 actions. That result identifies real work ahead. [Evidence](/evidence/echo4/WORLD1-20260921.md).

E4-SENSATION-1 starts by asking whether a sequence distinguishes situations that look identical in a single observation. We will compare current observations with histories of 2 and 4 steps, including executed actions, measuring prediction, remaining ambiguity and memory cost.

WSP retains its 16 bytes and field layout. Implementation will continue with integer arithmetic, bounded memory and cortex off. The sensory contract will determine available data; the next module is not implemented yet.

## If every stage turns green, what could be most exciting?

**Observing shared history usefully change how two agents understand each other and act.** If this effect transfers to new situations and disappears when relevant memory is removed, we would have causal evidence for that history's role.

Within the evaluated domain, we could demonstrate:

- An agent adjusting communication to the information available to its counterpart.
- Distinguishing a bodily fault from external change while retaining valid knowledge.
- Remembering a partner usefully and revising expectations when its behaviour changes.
- Reorganising roles when another agent has better information or capabilities.
- Reciprocal interaction contributing measurably beyond recordings or comparable individual strategies.

Imagine replaying an encounter, removing part of the memory and observing exactly what is lost. Or retaining that memory, changing both agents' capabilities and discovering what they learned about the relationship and what depended on context. **Building a laboratory where these questions have reproducible answers is an ambition worth pursuing.**

Results could inform future cooperation technologies and adaptive robots. Those applications would need their own evaluations.

## What would support the conclusion

We will use controls, held-out scenarios and criteria fixed before evaluation. Communication will be assessed by intervening on signals and checking effects; reward improvements alone do not explain the mechanism. [Lowe and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.05168).

This line has precedents, including systems modelling other agents from their behaviour. [Machine Theory of Mind](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07740). ECHO-4's specific commitment is to integrate body, continuity and shared history in its architecture and measure each part's contribution.

Green closure would support those functional capacities within the examined scope. Subjective consciousness would require additional evidence. The philosophical question stays open, and each experiment can sharpen what we are managing to explain.

**Development begins with a small question: what can ECHO distinguish from what happens to it? The direction is ambitious: understanding how that perspective changes on meeting another.**
